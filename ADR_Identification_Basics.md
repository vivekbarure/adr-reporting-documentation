# ADR Identification Basics

## Pharmacovigilance and Drug Safety --- Educational Project

**Project title:** ADR Identification Basics\
**Subject:** Pharmacovigilance\
**Purpose:** To learn the basic process of identifying, documenting, and
reporting suspected Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs).

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Introduction

An Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR) is a harmful and unintended response to a
medicine when it is used at normal doses for prevention, diagnosis, or
treatment of a disease. Pharmacovigilance is the science and activities
related to detecting, assessing, understanding, and preventing adverse
effects or other medicine-related problems.

## 2. Objectives

-   Understand the meaning of an ADR.
-   Recognize possible signs and symptoms of suspected ADRs.
-   Distinguish serious reactions from non-serious reactions.
-   Learn what information should be documented.
-   Understand the basic process for reporting suspected ADRs.
-   Encourage patient safety and rational medicine use.

## 3. Common examples

  Medicine      Possible adverse reaction
  ------------- -------------------------------------------------------
  Amoxicillin   Skin rash or allergic reaction
  Ibuprofen     Stomach irritation; rarely, gastrointestinal bleeding
  Amlodipine    Ankle swelling
  Metformin     Nausea or diarrhea

These are examples only. A symptom occurring after a medicine is taken
does not automatically prove that the medicine caused it.

## 4. How to identify a suspected ADR

1.  **Recognize the event:** Ask about new or worsening symptoms after a
    medicine was started, stopped, or changed.
2.  **Collect details:** Record the medicine name, dose, route, start
    date, time of reaction, symptoms, and relevant medical history.
3.  **Check timing:** Determine whether the timing is compatible with a
    possible medicine-related reaction.
4.  **Consider other causes:** The illness itself, other medicines,
    allergies, or other factors may explain the symptoms.
5.  **Assess seriousness:** Look for death, a life-threatening event,
    hospital admission or prolonged hospitalization, significant
    disability, or another medically important event.
6.  **Document objectively:** Record the patient's description and
    observed findings accurately; avoid assuming causality.
7.  **Report promptly:** Follow local reporting procedures and seek
    advice from a healthcare professional or pharmacovigilance centre.

## 5. Information to include in an ADR report

-   **Patient details:** A suitable patient identifier and relevant
    age/sex information, according to local privacy rules.
-   **Suspected medicine:** Name, dose, route, frequency, reason for
    use, and dates taken.
-   **Reaction details:** Signs/symptoms, onset date, severity, outcome,
    and treatment provided.
-   **Relevant history:** Known allergies, medical conditions, and other
    medicines.
-   **Reporter details:** Name and contact information of the healthcare
    professional or reporter, as required by the reporting system.
-   **Supporting information:** Laboratory results or medical records,
    where relevant and permitted.

Protect patient confidentiality. Do not include identifiable patient
information in a public GitHub repository.

## 6. Seriousness and warning signs

A suspected ADR may be serious if it results in death, is
life-threatening, requires or prolongs hospitalization, causes
significant disability, or is otherwise medically important.

**Seek emergency medical help** for warning signs such as difficulty
breathing, swelling of the face or throat, collapse, severe bleeding, or
a severe blistering rash. Do not delay urgent care to complete a report.
Patients should not stop essential prescribed treatment without medical
advice, except when emergency services direct otherwise.

## 7. Illustrative case study

**Scenario (fictional):** A patient starts a prescribed antibiotic and
develops a new itchy skin rash the following day. The patient reports no
breathing difficulty or facial swelling.

**Identification steps:** - Record the antibiotic name, dose, start
date, rash onset, and other medicines. - Ask about previous drug
allergies and other possible causes. - Arrange prompt assessment by a
qualified healthcare professional. - Document the reaction and
outcome. - Report it through the appropriate local pharmacovigilance
process.

**Important:** This is a suspected reaction only. The case does not
establish that the antibiotic caused the rash. If breathing difficulty
or facial/throat swelling occurs, seek emergency care immediately.

## 8. Basic ADR reporting workflow

**Recognize symptoms → Collect medicine and patient details → Assess
seriousness → Document accurately → Seek clinical assessment → Report
through the appropriate system → Follow up on outcome**

## 9. Conclusion

Early identification and accurate reporting of suspected ADRs help
healthcare professionals and medicine-safety programmes detect potential
risks. Every report should be factual, timely, confidential, and
submitted according to local requirements. A suspected ADR report is a
safety signal and does not, by itself, prove causation.
