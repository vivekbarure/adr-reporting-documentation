# Severity and Case Classification

## Pharmacovigilance and Drug Safety --- Educational Project

**Task:** Severity & Case Classification (Task 4)\
**Purpose:** To classify suspected adverse drug reactions (ADRs) by
severity and seriousness, and assess whether reactions are expected or
unexpected.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Introduction

Pharmacovigilance monitors the safety of medicines. When a suspected
adverse drug reaction is reported, the case should be described
accurately and assessed using the appropriate criteria. Severity,
seriousness, and expectedness are related but different concepts and
should not be used interchangeably.

## 2. Objectives

-   Understand severity and seriousness in ADR reporting.
-   Distinguish expected reactions from unexpected reactions.
-   Practice classifying fictional cases.
-   Recognize when urgent clinical assessment is needed.
-   Document the reasoning and uncertainty behind each classification.

## 3. Severity vs seriousness

**Severity** describes the intensity of a reaction, for example mild,
moderate, or severe.

-   **Mild:** Usually transient and causes little or no interference
    with normal activities.
-   **Moderate:** Causes discomfort or some interference with daily
    activities and may require treatment.
-   **Severe:** Intense or significantly disabling symptoms that may
    require urgent medical care.

**Seriousness** is a regulatory classification based on the outcome or
action required. A reaction is generally considered serious if it
results in death, is life-threatening, requires or prolongs
hospitalization, causes significant disability/incapacity, causes a
congenital anomaly/birth defect, or is otherwise medically important.

A reaction can be severe in intensity without meeting a regulatory
seriousness criterion, and a reaction that initially appears mild may
still need careful assessment.

## 4. Expected vs unexpected reaction

-   **Expected:** The nature and severity of the reaction are consistent
    with the applicable approved product information or reference safety
    information.
-   **Unexpected:** The nature, severity, or specificity of the reaction
    is inconsistent with that reference information.

Expectedness must be checked against the correct product information for
the medicine and the applicable reporting system. Do not decide
expectedness based on memory alone.

## 5. Fictional case studies

### Case 1: Mild, non-serious, expected (illustrative)

**Scenario:** A patient taking metformin reports mild nausea during the
first few days of treatment. The symptom is short-lived, does not
interfere significantly with normal activities, and no hospitalization
or other serious outcome occurs.

**Illustrative classification:** - Severity: Mild - Seriousness:
Non-serious based on the information provided - Expectedness:
Potentially expected; confirm against the applicable product information

**Reasoning:** Mild nausea can occur with metformin, but expectedness
must be verified using the appropriate reference information.

### Case 2: Severe and serious, potentially unexpected (illustrative)

**Scenario:** A patient taking a medicine develops difficulty breathing
and swelling of the face and throat and is taken to an emergency
department.

**Illustrative classification:** - Severity: Severe - Seriousness:
Serious; potentially life-threatening - Expectedness: Must be checked
against the medicine's applicable reference safety information

**Action:** This is a medical emergency. Seek emergency care
immediately; do not delay treatment to complete documentation.

### Case 3: Moderate, non-serious, expectedness unknown

**Scenario:** A patient develops a persistent headache after starting a
new medicine. The headache interferes with some daily activities, but no
hospitalization or other serious outcome is reported. The product
information is not available to the assessor.

**Illustrative classification:** - Severity: Moderate - Seriousness: No
seriousness criterion is described in the scenario; confirm with the
full case information - Expectedness: Unknown until the correct
reference safety information is checked

**Reasoning:** The available information is insufficient to classify
expectedness.

### Case 4: Serious due to hospitalization

**Scenario:** A patient develops a suspected medicine-related reaction
that requires hospital admission for monitoring and treatment.

**Illustrative classification:** - Severity: Assess from the intensity
of symptoms and clinical findings - Seriousness: Serious because
hospitalization was required - Expectedness: Check the applicable
product information

**Reasoning:** Hospitalization can meet a seriousness criterion even if
symptom intensity is not described as severe.

## 6. Basic classification workflow

1.  Record the suspected medicine, dose, dates, symptoms, and outcome.
2.  Describe severity based on symptom intensity and functional impact.
3.  Assess seriousness against recognized outcome/action criteria.
4.  Compare the reaction with the applicable reference safety
    information to assess expectedness.
5.  Document the evidence, missing information, and rationale.
6.  Escalate urgent symptoms for immediate medical care.
7.  Report through the appropriate local pharmacovigilance system and
    protect patient confidentiality.

## 8. Conclusion

Correct ADR classification requires separate assessment of severity,
seriousness, and expectedness. The report should state what is known,
what remains uncertain, and the evidence supporting each conclusion.
Classification supports medicine safety monitoring but does not, by
itself, prove that a medicine caused the reaction.
