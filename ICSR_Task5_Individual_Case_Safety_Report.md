# Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)

## Pharmacovigilance Case Assessment --- Educational Project

**Task:** Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) --- Task 5\
**Role simulated:** Drug Safety Associate\
**Purpose:** Demonstrate ADR identification, case documentation,
causality assessment, and severity/seriousness classification using a
fictional case.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Introduction

An Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) is a structured report of one or
more suspected adverse reactions in an individual patient after exposure
to a medicine. It supports pharmacovigilance by documenting safety
information in a consistent, timely, and confidential manner.

This is a fictional educational exercise. It is not a real patient
report and must not be submitted to a regulator as an actual case.

## 2. Fictional case scenario

A 42-year-old adult is prescribed amoxicillin for a bacterial infection.
On the second day of treatment, the patient develops an itchy skin rash.
The patient contacts a healthcare professional. No breathing difficulty,
facial swelling, collapse, or hospital admission is reported in the
initial information. The patient's other medicines, allergy history,
clinical examination, final diagnosis, and outcome have not yet been
confirmed.

**Important:** This scenario describes a suspected association only. It
does not establish that amoxicillin caused the rash.

## 4. ADR identification

The rash appeared after the medicine was started, so a medicine-related
reaction is possible. However, the infection itself, another medicine,
an allergy, or another condition may also explain the rash. The case
should be documented as a **suspected ADR**, pending clinical assessment
and further information.

## 5. Causality assessment

**Preliminary category: Possible (provisional educational assessment).**

**Rationale:** - The event occurred after exposure to the suspected
medicine. - A rash can be associated with antibiotic use. - Alternative
explanations have not been ruled out. - Dechallenge information, medical
assessment, allergy history, and outcome are unavailable.

The category may change when additional reliable information becomes
available. The assessor should use the causality method required by the
applicable organisation or reporting system.

## 6. Severity, seriousness, and expectedness

-   **Severity:** Cannot be confidently graded from the limited
    description. The intensity, extent of rash, and impact on daily
    activities need assessment.
-   **Seriousness:** No seriousness criterion is described in the
    initial scenario, but the full case must be checked. A reaction is
    generally serious if it results in death, is life-threatening,
    requires or prolongs hospitalization, causes significant disability,
    causes a congenital anomaly, or is otherwise medically important.
-   **Expectedness:** Must be checked against the applicable amoxicillin
    product information/reference safety information. Do not classify it
    as expected or unexpected without reviewing the correct reference.

**Safety warning:** Rash accompanied by difficulty breathing, swelling
of the face or throat, collapse, or severe blistering/peeling requires
urgent medical evaluation. Do not delay emergency care to complete a
report.

## 7. Follow-up questions

1.  What were the exact dose, route, frequency, and treatment dates?
2.  What was the exact onset date and time of the rash?
3.  Was the rash localized or widespread, and were there other symptoms?
4.  Does the patient have a history of penicillin or other drug
    allergies?
5.  What other medicines or supplements were being taken?
6.  Was the medicine stopped or changed by a healthcare professional,
    and what happened afterward?
7.  Was medical treatment, an emergency visit, or hospitalization
    required?
8.  What was the final diagnosis and outcome?
9.  What is the reporter's role and what is the source of the
    information?

## 8. Reporting workflow

1.  Receive and validate the initial report.
2.  Check the minimum information required by the applicable reporting
    system.
3.  Record patient, suspected medicine, reaction, and reporter
    information accurately.
4.  Assess seriousness and identify urgent safety concerns.
5.  Perform preliminary causality and expectedness assessments using the
    required method and reference information.
6.  Request follow-up for missing details.
7.  Submit through the appropriate authorised pharmacovigilance channel
    within applicable timelines.
8.  Update the case when new information arrives and retain an audit
    trail.

## 9. Privacy and data quality

-   Use only the minimum patient information necessary.
-   Do not publish names, phone numbers, addresses, medical record
    numbers, or other identifying details in a public repository.
-   Clearly label fictional or training data.
-   Do not guess unknown dates, doses, outcomes, or diagnoses.
-   Keep the assessment factual and separate reported facts from
    professional interpretation.

## 10. Conclusion

This fictional case demonstrates how an ICSR can document a suspected
rash following amoxicillin exposure. The initial information is
incomplete, so causality is only provisionally assessed as Possible;
severity and expectedness require further information, and seriousness
should be verified. Accurate documentation, follow-up, confidentiality,
and timely reporting are essential parts of pharmacovigilance.
